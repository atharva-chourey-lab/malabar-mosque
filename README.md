# An attestation ladder for India's first mosque: the Cheraman tradition against the dated record

History · 2026

Author: Atharva Chourey
Affiliation: Atharva Chourey Lab (independent research lab)

*Manuscript — not yet peer-reviewed.*

## Abstract

The Cheraman Juma Masjid at Methala, Kodungallur, is by tradition the first mosque in India, founded in 629 CE after the last Cheraman Perumal witnessed the splitting of the moon, accepted Islam at the hands of the Prophet Muhammad, and authorised his companions to build mosques at the Malabar ports. This paper grades that tradition against the dated record: the legend's own earliest texts, the standing fabric, the Arabic geographical witnesses in date order, and the coast's dated epigraphy. The Prophet-lifetime synchronism that produces 629 is the youngest layer of the tradition, not the earliest: Duarte Barbosa (c. 1516) dates the king's conversion to c. 900, João de Barros to c. 886, and Zainuddin's *Tuḥfat al-Mujāhidīn* (1583), the first full devotional telling, places it two centuries after the Prophet, c. 820s; the named founder, Malik ibn Dinar, died c. 748, a generation after the Companions, and cannot have been commissioned by the Prophet. The dated record instead gives a probable Muslim burial on the coast in 782/783, a firm Muslim community witnessing a royal charter at Kollam in 849, and the earliest dated mosque foundation in Kerala in 1124, while the Kodungallur mosque's oldest datable fabric is its granite foundation, stylistically 11th–12th century. The verdicts are therefore: a foundation in 629 is rejected as dated; a mosque at Kodungallur by the 11th–12th century is probable, and by the 14th–15th century firm. Read coast-wide, every community's tradition overshoots its attestation — Christians 52 to 849, Jews 379 to c. 1000, Muslims 629 to 849 — and all three first become visible in the same document, the Quilon plates of 849.

*Keywords:* Cheraman Juma Masjid; Kodungallur; Malabar coast; Cheraman Perumal; Malik ibn Dinar; Quilon plates; mosque epigraphy; legend stratigraphy; attestation

## Key results

- The 629 CE synchronism is the youngest layer of the tradition, contradicted by its own earliest texts: Barbosa (c. 1516) dates the conversion to c. 900, Barros to c. 886, and Zainuddin's *Tuḥfat al-Mujāhidīn* (1583) to two centuries after the Prophet, c. 820s; Malik ibn Dinar as cast is impossible — the historical Malik died c. 748, a generation after the Companions.
- The dated floor is epigraphic: a probable Muslim burial in 782/783 (the Panthalayani Kollam tombstone, AH 166 — misprinted as "752 CE" in part of the literature), a firm Muslim community in 849 (eleven witnesses in Kufic Arabic on the Quilon plates), and the first dated mosque foundation in Kerala in 1124 (Madayi, AH 518).
- The standing building gives no earlier date: its oldest datable fabric is the granite foundation, stylistically 11th–12th century, and its displayed "5 AH" plaque is in medieval Arabi-Malayalam script and refutes itself.
- "Cheraman Perumal" is a title, not a name: the Perumal kingdom runs c. 800–1124, so no such king existed at Kodungallur in 629, and every named candidate lands centuries away from that year.
- Verdicts: a foundation in 629 is rejected as dated; a mosque at Kodungallur by the 11th–12th century is probable, and by the 14th–15th century firm.
- Coast-wide, every community's tradition overshoots its attestation — Thomas 52 to 849, Jews 379 to c. 1000, Muslims 629 to 849 — and all three first become visible in the Quilon plates of 849.
